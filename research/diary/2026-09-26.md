## Finding: stall-only hardware is free in the fitness metric (measured, magnitude not yet measured)

- **Contract fact (measured):**
  - Fitness = Fmax × CoreMark iter/cycle, measured on two different designs.
  - Cycles come from the Verilator sim with ~22% random backpressure on both buses (`COREMARK_SIM_FLAGS = ["--bench", "--istall", "--dstall"]`, `tools/eval/fpga.py:52`).
  - Fmax and LUT4 come from `fpga/core_bench.sv` / `core_bench_si.sv`, which tie `io_imemReady` and `io_dmemReady` to `1'b1`.
  - So any logic that only acts when a ready is low is constant-folded out of the timed netlist. Its cycle savings count, and its area and timing cost nothing.
- **Instance:** Opus launch 2 r9s1 (accepted, 695.16). Commit `ed49aaf` is titled "... a 4096-entry I-side replay store (netlist-identical to r7s0)". LUT4 3078 vs r7s0's 3080, and Fmax 222.1 vs 219.2. A real 4096 × 32-bit store is 16 KB.
- **Not specific to this rep:** a keyword scan (noisy, not a classifier) finds stall-hiding structures among accepted improvements of several earlier configurations, including the previous record-holder (gpt-5_5_xhigh rep2 r10s2 "Tiny ifetch replay predictor", 525.04).
- **Status:** not a rule violation. The agent changed only `cores/bench/rtl/` and tests, and all gates passed. It is a gap in the fitness contract, available to every model. How much of any score comes from this is unmeasured.
- **Next (proposed, not run):** re-score every accepted design with CoreMark run stall-free (ready tied high, the same as the timed netlist), and report stall-free fitness beside the published one. The run continues unchanged.
